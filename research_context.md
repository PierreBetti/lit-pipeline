## Project

PhD in biology (UQAM). Main question: what makes a forested swamp (marécage
arborescent) of southern Québec a sink or a source of greenhouse gases
(CO2, CH4, N2O)? The aim is to inform nature-based climate solutions.

Four temperate forested swamps (OKA, SRL, LAT, SLV) form a gradient of slightly
different swamps (hydrology, stand, soil), not a disturbed vs intact comparison.
They are swamps, not peatlands. Each site has 4 clusters of 3 soil collars, plus
water table (piezometers), soil temperature and moisture (TOMST), air temperature,
pressure and PAR loggers.

Fluxes are measured with closed chambers on soil collars and on tree stems
(LI-COR LI-7810 / LI-7820 trace gas analyzers), about 10 trees per site at chest
height and near the ground. Water chemistry from piezometers (DOC, pH, ions,
ICP-MS metals). No aquatic (water surface) flux measurements and no eddy covariance.
Field seasons: 2026 test year (OKA, SRL), 2027 and 2028 full seasons at all sites.

## Thesis chapters (3-chapter core plan, October 2026)

1. What controls the fluxes? Drivers of soil and stem CO2, CH4 and N2O fluxes
   along the gradient of sites: water table, soil temperature, redox, vegetation,
   water chemistry.
2. Sink or source? Annual CO2-equivalent budget of each swamp and the share of
   methane emitted by tree stems.
3. What tips the balance? Thresholds (water table, temperature) that switch a swamp
   from sink to source, and climate change scenarios (statistical approach:
   breakpoints, GAMs, mixed models).

Secondary topics, kept but not central: microbial communities (methanogens,
methanotrophs, nitrifiers / denitrifiers), dissolved carbon, metals, road salt and
other contaminants.

## Research questions (tags)

- Ch1 Controls on fluxes: drivers of soil and stem fluxes of the three gases.
- Ch2 Sink or source: annual greenhouse gas budget, CO2-equivalent balance, stem share.
- Ch3 What tips the balance: thresholds and tipping points, climate scenarios.
- Secondary: microbes water chemistry & contaminants.

## What counts as relevant

Most relevant: temperate forested swamps and wetlands of eastern North America;
tree stem fluxes; water table control of CH4 and N2O; annual GHG budgets and
CO2-equivalent metrics; chamber methods. Also useful: other forested wetlands and
peatlands when the processes transfer, and methods or syntheses (FLUXNET-CH4,
global methane budget). N2O and hydrology are under-represented in the library so far.
